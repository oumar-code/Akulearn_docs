## Purpose
This document describes the high‑level corporate structure, ownership, governance and commercial relationships across the Aku Platform ecosystem. It establishes that each service operates as an independent operating company (OpCo) with its own profit & loss responsibility while Aku Platform Holdings designs, builds and operates the shared network of AI‑native services and IP.

## Shareholding (Holdings company)
- Saudatu Sa'idu Bungudu — 75% of Aku Platform Holdings (majority).
- Abubakar Na'allah Iliyasu — 25% of Aku Platform Holdings (minority).

## High‑level model
- Aku Platform Holdings Ltd (the "Holdings")
  - Role: apex technology & treasury vehicle that designs, builds, licenses and operates AI native platform components, core IP, R&D and group standards.
  - R&D company: registered in Nigeria and housed under Holdings.
  - Commercial model: Holdings receives a target 30%–40% equity stake in each OpCo in exchange for IP, platform integration, and shared services. In addition, OpCos will enter into Technology Licence and Shared Services Agreements (fees/royalties to be contractually defined).
- Each Service = an independent OpCo
  - Each service listed below will be a separate legal operating company, registered in Nigeria, with its own board, management team and full P&L responsibility.
  - Holdings takes 30–40% equity in each OpCo (economic share + ongoing commercial terms for licensing & platform services). The remaining equity in each OpCo is held by founders, local investors, employees or other investors as agreed per‑OpCo.

## Corporate governance & control highlights
- Board composition (Holdings)
  - Holdings board reflects majority shareholder control (Saudatu 75%). Board size and committee structure to be set by shareholders; default recommendations below.
- OpCo governance
  - Each OpCo has its own board and management; Holdings, as a 30–40% shareholder and IP licensor, retains:
    - a minimum governance seat (e.g., 1 director or observer)
    - defined reserved‑matter rights in the shareholders’ agreement to protect group IP, standards and intercompany arrangements.
- Reserved Matters (examples requiring supermajority / negotiated approval)
  - Amendment of OpCo constitutional documents affecting Holdings’ IP or licensing.
  - Issuance/dilution of shares (pre‑emption rights for Holdings).
  - Sale/transfer of material IP or the OpCo’s core assets.
  - Entering material related‑party contracts contrary to group policy.
  - Change of OpCo business scope outside agreed service boundaries.
- Decision rights for technical standards
  - Holdings defines and maintains core technical standards, model governance, and security controls. OpCos must comply with the Group Technology Standards (documented and versioned) as a condition of licence.

## Inter‑company agreements
- Technology Licence Agreement (TLA)
  - Holdings retains ownership of core platform IP, models, toolkits and licenses them to OpCos; fees/royalties and scope defined per OpCo.
- Shared Services Agreement (SSA)
  - Holdings (or a designated Shared Services OpCo) provides platform ops, SRE, model hosting, data engineering and MLOps to OpCos for agreed fees.
- Manufacturing/Delivery / Operational Service Agreements (MSA/OSA)
  - Where OpCos provide services to other OpCos (e.g., EdgeHub content packaging for Akudemy), formal service agreements define transfer pricing and invoicing.
- Inter‑Company Logistics & Content Supply (ICLA)
  - For content distribution, hardware provisioning and logistics between OpCos.

## Financial & treasury policies
- Each OpCo maintains separate accounting, profit & loss and local tax filings (registered in Nigeria).
- Treasury & FX: Holdings manages group cash pooling and FX hedging where applicable.
- Dividend / Remittance policy: OpCos remit an agreed percentage of distributable profits to shareholders after local reinvestment obligations; exact percentage determined by board and local laws.
- Transfer Pricing: All inter‑company transactions priced at arm’s length and documented to meet tax authority requirements.

## Conflicts, IP & compliance
- Conflicts of interest must be disclosed. Directors with direct conflicts shall abstain from related votes.
- Holdings remains the canonical owner of core R&D and IP developed under group R&D programs; OpCos may develop local enhancements that are licensed back (terms negotiated).
- Compliance: all entities comply with Nigerian corporate & tax law and applicable sector regulation.


## Dispute resolution & amendment
- Good faith negotiation, then mediation within 30 days if unresolved. Failing mediation, binding arbitration in Nigeria under agreed rules (or courts of Nigeria if arbitration unenforceable).
- This document or the shareholders’ agreement may be amended per the thresholds set out in the shareholders’ agreement (typical supermajority for material changes).
